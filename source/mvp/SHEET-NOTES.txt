XOR XPU: finite 2-adic probability-sheet experiment

Every computed XOR bit remains binary: black XOR black = black; black XOR
white = white; white XOR black = white; white XOR white = black. Spin labels
are classical simulated values, s = 1 - 2*bit, so 0 maps to +1 and 1 to -1.

The model samples a finite batch whose coordinates are (committed epoch,
worker address). Each committed epoch is a shared descriptor transition over
the same initialized RAM base bits. One virtual XOR per stored worker address
is represented at each transition. CPU and GPU explicitly calculate selected
pairs. The represented rate is domain size * committed transitions / actual
pipeline wall seconds. It is separate from reconstruction sample throughput.

Sampling is spread over numerical address regions and contiguous epoch
regions, then separated into worker-address residue classes modulo 2^depth.
The residue classes form a finite 2-adic hierarchy because closer 2-adic
addresses share more low-order bits. Ordinary adjacent numerical addresses
need not be neighbors in that metric. Counts use exact finite populations,
including uneven edge regions and domains that are not powers of two.

Uniform ordered sampling without replacement selects training and disjoint
holdout coordinates in each terminal cell. The field seed remains fixed and
recorded. A separate independently generated sampling seed is recorded and
preserved so a sample design can be replayed. Confidence statements assume
the reproducible PRNG behaves as the ideal uniform sampling design; they do
not require independent field bits or assert quantum randomness.

For K terminal cells, alpha = 1 - requested confidence and n training samples,
half the failure budget is assigned to all leaves and hierarchy parents and
half to the separate global mean interval: alpha_leaf = alpha_global = alpha/2.
Each cell uses epsilon = sqrt(log(2*K/alpha_leaf)/(2*n)). The estimated fraction of
white XOR outcomes has interval [max(0,p-epsilon), min(1,p+epsilon)]. A fully
enumerated cell has an exact mean interval. Spin mean = 1 - 2*p, with interval
[1 - 2*upperProbability, 1 - 2*lowerProbability]. Hoeffding's finite-population
bound and a union bound give simultaneous coverage under the stated sampling
assumption. Exact population weights propagate intervals to hierarchy parents
and the global mean. These conservative aggregate intervals can be broad.
The global mean also receives a tighter independently derived stratified bound:
epsilon_global = sqrt(log(2/alpha_global)*sum_nonexhaustive((N_h/N)^2/n_h)/2).
Exhaustively trained strata contribute zero uncertainty. This global bound
uses ideal independence of sampling designs across the disjoint strata. The
leaf and global failure budgets sum to alpha, so the combined guarantee is at
least the requested confidence. Both the propagated leaf interval and the
tighter global interval are exported; neither is an exact binary output.

Holdout results do not enter fitted probabilities or intervals. Their Brier
scores compare probability predictions with actual disjoint binary outcomes.
A constant 0.5 predictor has Brier score 0.25. These are observed validation
scores, not proof of all unseen outputs. Exhaustively trained cells may have
no remaining holdouts; no holdout result is fabricated for those cells.

There are three different returns:
  1. Actual selected CPU/GPU XOR bits and simulated spin labels.
  2. A complete queryable piecewise probability-sheet table with intervals.
  3. An exact known-equation descriptor containing field seed, initial base
     pattern, worker domain and committed epoch range. It computes any
     requested individual output from the supplied generator equation.

The exact descriptor is a compact formula, not statistically recovered
unseen bits. A dense full batch return still requires generating and writing
each output. For an arbitrary N-bit field, m observed coordinates leave
2^(N-m) assignments compatible with those observations. The current seeded
hash field has no assumed p-adic smoothness or sparse basis representation;
its probability fit can perform no better than an unstructured random field.

The term 2-adic here denotes the explicit low-bit multiresolution model. This
experiment does not implement a p-adic bulk action, a conformal field theory,
a Bruhat-Tits holographic tensor network, or physical spin measurements. XOR
spin correlations alone do not specify complex quantum amplitudes, unitary
circuits, measurement bases or entanglement. The simulator reports its
classical measurements, mathematical model and represented counts separately.

The independent audit enumerates bounded small domains, checks the exact
population partition and sample uniqueness, independently calculates the
confidence intervals and weighted hierarchy, proves fitting has no holdout
leakage, and compares the query descriptor with a separately transcribed
scalar oracle. Its live test allocates only 4 MiB and checks actual CPU/GPU
sampling, zero added epoch credit, seed preservation, replay and release.
It also pauses immediately after a genuinely completed CPU/GPU query chunk,
verifies that completed work stays counted while later chunks stop, verifies
pre-signaled pause and reserve-floor checks prevent additional reads, and
confirms a resumed query reconstructs the same seed, coordinates and outcomes.

Recorded full-reservoir experiment (2026-10-09)

The actual root experiment initialized 12,769,820,672 bytes once, providing
102,158,565,376 stored bit addresses. It individually committed 5,644 shared
epoch transitions in 2.000181099996553 measured pipeline wall seconds. That
represents 576,582,942,982,144 virtual XOR points in the batch, or approximately
288.265 trillion represented XOR transformations per actual wall second.
The rate can vary with available RAM, process activity and sample timing; the
earlier approximately 345.7 trillion figure was from a different recorded run.

The epoch window explicitly calculated 361,216 primary sampled XOR pairs:
180,608 on CPU and 180,608 on GPU. Subsequent reconstruction explicitly
calculated 10,240 additional selected pairs, of which 4,788 were CPU and 5,452
GPU. The sheet used 128 terminal cells, each with 64 training and 16 disjoint
holdout samples: 8,192 training and 2,048 holdout points in total. Reconstruction
took 1.6824037999977008 seconds including design, real RAM reads, CPU/GPU query
completion, statistics and sheet formatting, and earned zero new virtual
epoch credit. No full-reservoir writes occurred during these epochs or queries.

Estimated global white probability was 0.4969482421875. Its jointly allocated
confidence interval was [0.4805941087546647, 0.5133023756203353]. The estimated
global simulated spin mean was 0.006103515625, with interval
[-0.026604751240670632, 0.03881178249067063]. These describe the estimated
finite-population mean under the documented design assumptions.

Held-out bit prediction accuracy was 48.876953125%. Held-out Brier score was
0.2541980743408203, compared with 0.25 for a constant half-probability model
(smaller is better). This batch therefore did not demonstrate improved
individual-bit prediction over the unstructured half-probability baseline.
The sheet provides probability estimates and a complete known-generator query
descriptor; its sparse samples did not recover the unseen binary field.

Independent full-report parsing checked all 10,240 actual outputs against a
separately transcribed scalar XOR generator, replayed every preserved sampling
coordinate, recomputed leaf and global confidence intervals and weighted
holdout metrics, checked exact native source hashes, and verified operation
counts, fixed field and sampling seed records and full native RAM release.
Its 34 checks passed. The separate live small-memory integration audit passed
29 checks including replay, cancellation, reserve-floor behavior and release.
